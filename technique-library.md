# BJJ Technique Library

Nov 17, 2026 · @Jason

## How to use this list

Each section is a starting position; each row is a technique that ends in one of four outcomes. Gi-only techniques are marked (gi). Add rows as you learn new variations.

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Sub | Submission (choke, joint lock, compression) |
| Sweep | Bottom player reverses to top |
| Pass | Top player clears the legs (to side, mount, KOB, back) |
| Transition | Change of position or guard without a full reversal |

## Leg locks

Each leg entanglement position has several finishes, plus sweeps and moves into other positions when the finish isn't there. Legality depends on belt and ruleset (IBJJF gi bans heel hooks and reaping below brown/black), so check your gym's rules.

### Straight ashi garami (single leg X family)

One leg trapped, your outside foot on their hip, inside leg across the back of their thigh, their foot in your armpit.

| Option | Outcome | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight ankle lock (Achilles lock) | Sub | Blade of the forearm under the Achilles, arch back |
| Outside heel hook | Sub | Their heel turns out of your armpit; turn toward their far side |
| Toe hold | Sub | Figure-four on the foot, rotate toward their butt |
| Kneebar | Sub | Slide up the leg when they turn away or stand |
| Estima lock | Sub | Foot lock with the foot pinned to your hip |
| Kick-over / technical stand-up sweep | Sweep | Kick the hip, come up on the leg |
| To inside sankaku (saddle) | Transition | Swing inside leg across their hip |
| To 50-50 | Transition | When they bring the free leg over |
| To cross ashi | Transition | Switch your outside leg to the inside |

### Outside ashi (outside sankaku)

Like straight ashi, but your legs triangle on the outside of their hip, limiting their rotation.

### Inside sankaku / saddle (honey hole, 411)

Your legs triangle between their legs, controlling the near leg and blocking the far leg.

| Option | Outcome | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside heel hook | Sub | Highest-percentage finish |
| Outside heel hook | Sub | When they turn to escape the inside heel hook |
| Toe hold | Sub |  |

| Calf slicer | Sub | Uncommon from here |
| Back take | Transition | They turn to defend; follow the back |
| Sweep to top / stand up | Sweep | Come up holding the leg |
| To 50-50 or cross ashi | Transition | When they clear your triangle |

## Classic chains and combinations

Each chain uses the opponent's defense to the first move to set up the next.

1. Hip bump sweep → kimura → guillotine (closed guard)
2. Armbar → triangle → omoplata (closed guard attack triangle)
3. Cross collar choke → armbar (gi closed guard)
4. Scissor sweep → flower sweep (pressure changes direction)
5. Omoplata → sweep to top if they roll → kimura / straight armlock
6. Kimura → back take when they hide the arm
7. Old school sweep → knee lever (half guard)
8. Underhook (dogfight) → back take or single leg (half guard)
9. Butterfly sweep → X-guard → technical stand-up sweep
10. DLR → berimbolo → back take → rear naked choke
11. Toreando → knee cut → mount (passing chain)
12. Knee cut → backstep when they underhook → leg entanglement
13. Leg drag → back take → choke
14. Side control → knee on belly → far side armbar
15. Side control → mount → S-mount → armbar
16. Mount: Americana → armbar when they push → triangle when they free the arm
17. Mount → gift wrap → back take → rear naked choke
18. Back: RNC → armbar when they fight hands
19. Back: collar choke → bow and arrow (gi)
20. Snap down → front headlock → darce / anaconda / guillotine
21. Single leg X → straight ankle lock → kick-over sweep if they escape
22. Mount escape: upa → elbow-knee if they post wide
